## Summary of the mass gap report (2026-11-09)

- The mass gap problem asks for a proof that the lightest excitation of a certain field theory has a mass above zero, even though the underlying waves are massless. It is unsolved. Numerical work puts that lightest state at about 1.5 to 1.8 GeV.
- About 1% of a proton's mass is the rest mass of its quarks. The rest is energy of motion and of the field. Mainstream physics already treats most ordinary mass as bound energy.
- Liquid helium stays liquid near absolute zero because the motion of its atoms outweighs the weak attraction between them. Its lowest excitations cost almost no energy, which makes it a different case from a medium with a gap.
- Bearing on this work: the theory's claim that mass is bound energy is not in dispute. What the theory has to supply is the mechanism that traps the energy and the mechanism that makes it attract.
