# Failure ledger

A missed target or a failed prediction is a result, not a bug. Every miss gets an entry here, with the same depth as a hit. The `scholar` worker writes the entries. The orchestrator supplies the expected value, the measured value, the ranked causes and the diagnostics. The scholar adds the literature check.

A failing code check is a different thing (an engineering problem, handled by the rules in `CLAUDE.md`) and does not go here unless it turns into a missed prediction.

## Entry format

Entries are appended in order. Newest last. Each entry uses this template.

```
### F-NNN  Short title
- Date, session, work package, step:
- Prediction on record (with the page and section it comes from):
- Expected value or outcome, with its tolerance:
- Measured value or outcome, with parameters (beta, omega, N, grid, box, T) and the result file path:
- Size of the miss against the tolerance (target, within about 3 times, beyond):
- Ranked causes (orchestrator): 1 ... 2 ... 3 ...
- Diagnostics run now (up to three cheap ones), what each returned:
- Diagnostics proposed (expensive ones), not run:
- What the miss rules in, and what it rules out:
- Literature check (scholar): has published work already seen and explained this? Sources with links and tags
  (READ, ABSTRACT, SECONDHAND) and the searches run. Write "no source found" where that is the case.
- Cross-reference: PRIOR_ART.md section, SESSION report section, reviewer finding if any:
- Status: open | explained | rerun not needed | superseded
```

## Rules for entries

- No rerunning to rescue a result. The entry records what happened and why.
- Numbers carry their parameters. A number from a different potential, dimension or amplitude regime is context, not a prediction, and is labelled so.
- Literature claims follow the evidence rules in `PRIOR_ART.md`: a link and one tag per claim, and "no source found" when nothing turned up.
- If a miss was already anticipated in `PRIOR_ART.md` (for example, the unstable-branch radial run settling onto the stable branch instead of dispersing, or a Step 2 energy retention below 90% near a radiation peak), the entry says so and links the section.

## Entries

Entries add one field to the template, "Interpretation notes (scholar)", for points that change how the miss should be read. The orchestrator's text is kept as supplied; scholar additions are marked.

### F-001  Absorber validation (WP1B check 4): free Gaussian in an L = 50 box does not match the L = 100 reference

- Date, session, work package, step: 2026-10-01, Session 1, WP1B (engine v2), check 4. It gates the box design for Step 2 (plan section 4, "Parameter selection for Step 2", Box bullet).
- Prediction on record: `docs/SWIRL_PLAN_2026-10-01.md`, Appendix A, WP1B check 4; section 5 standard 4 (absorbing boundaries validated against a box twice the size).
- Expected value or outcome, with its tolerance: relative L2 error at most 0.05 over r < 10 at every sampled time up to T = 75, for a free Gaussian (Set B: m = 1, g4 = 0.30, g6 = 0.055, phi0 = 0.5, R = 2.5) in an L = 50 periodic box with the absorber on, against an L = 100 run with no absorber. The plan adds "if L = 50 cannot reach it, report the smallest box that does", so it allowed for this outcome. The entry exists because of the size of the miss and because its mechanism bears on the Step 2 box.
- Measured value or outcome: result file `outputs/engine/check4_absorber.json` (worktree agent-ad6ebac785fbd2772); design scan `outputs/engine/absorber_design_scan.json`; test `tests/engine/test_check4_absorber.py`. Absorber `SPONGE_DEFAULT`: spherical gamma(r) phi_t, gamma_max = 2, width 12, cubic ramp, r_in = L/2 - 12. dx = 0.78125, dt = dx/8, T = 75.
  - L = 50: phi-only relative L2 max 1.744 at T = 75 (0.559 at t = 66 was the first figure quoted; the maximum is the honest headline). State-norm metric sqrt(|phi|^2 + |phi_t|^2) max 0.463. Error over initial norm 0.060. The reference norm on r < 10 falls to 3 to 6 percent of its initial value at the late samples, so the phi-only metric is ill-conditioned there. Error stays below 0.5 percent until t of about 36.

  - Sanity: L = 125 with the absorber on against the reference, 3e-9.
- Size of the miss against the tolerance: at L = 50, 35 times the target on the phi-only metric and 9 times on the state-norm metric (beyond about 3 times). At L = 62.5: 4.6 times phi-only (beyond), 1.2 times state-norm (within, pass with note). L = 68.75 passes.
- Ranked causes (author and reviewer): (1) Low and moderate k components (k about 0.6, group speed about 0.5) reflect off the sponge front. Onset times scale with r_in: first 1e-3 error at t = 35.5, 48.2, 58.4, 73.6 for r_in = 13, 16.1, 19.25, 22.4. (2) The interior is smaller than the dispersing slow packet, so the sponge damps the packet itself. (3) Metric conditioning.
- Diagnostics run now: box scan above. Onset times against r_in. L = 100 with the L = 50 sponge geometry gives the same numbers to 4 digits, so periodic wrap is ruled out. Sponge strength at L = 50: phi-only max 0.34, 0.28, 0.70, 1.74, 2.94 for gamma_max 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4; best of a 28-variant scan 0.163 (3.3 times target). Fast packet: for a k0 = 2.4 packet the default sponge leaves 0.0021 of the state norm and a weak sponge leaves 0.287, so weak sponges are not a fix for Step 2.
- Diagnostics proposed (expensive ones), not run: none supplied by the orchestrator. Scholar suggestions (cheap, from the literature check below): (a) add wide, weak designs (widths 25 to 40, gamma_max 0.3 to 1) to the 1D scan `swirl/engine/sponge1d.py`, which now covers widths 8 to 14 and gamma_max 0.3 to 4, and report the returned fraction for k0 between 0.2 and 1.0; (b) judge a Step 2 box by the published test, energy in a shell against total energy while an oscillon sits on its plateau, rather than relative L2 on a decaying free tail.
- What the miss rules in, and what it rules out: Rules in: the interior radius must be about 22 or more for slow free packets observed to T = 75 with this sponge; Step 2 boxes and the linear twin must use a large interior. Rules out: fast or third-harmonic reflection and periodic wrap as the cause.
- Literature check (scholar): Seen before and explained qualitatively; not quantified for this sponge. [Honda's thesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0009104) (READ) says the massive Klein-Gordon equation has no closed-form outgoing condition because the radiation speed is not unique, so sponge-type conditions are approximate and can back-scatter, and [Honda & Choptuik 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/0110065) (READ) say Sommerfeld conditions reflect significantly for massive fields. [Gleiser & Sornborger 2000](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9909002) (READ) use the same gamma(rho) phi_t sponge as the engine for 3D phi^4 oscillons, start the damping at about 20 times the oscillon scale, validate by shell energy (r < 5 R_0) to better than 1e-4 against an expanding grid, and report that long-wavelength modes are damped less efficiently than short ones and reflect from the far end of the layer, curing it with a larger grid and a later start of damping. Their layer is about 150 wide with gamma at the far end of about 0.6 against m = 1.4 (DERIVED from the extracted formula), roughly 5 times weaker relative to m and 12 times wider than the engine default. [Li, Yamaguchi, Zhang 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13276) (READ, App. B) use the same form for the beta = 4/3 sextic model with damping starting at r = 64. Every published radial run I read puts the absorber 50 to 100 from the core (PRIOR_ART section 6 and Addendum A1). Even a perfectly matched layer for nonlinear Klein-Gordon ([Antoine & Zhao 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.02528), READ) has an in-layer attenuation factor that tends to zero as the mode frequency approaches the mass (DERIVED from their eq. 2.5), and the free 3D Klein-Gordon field decays only as t^(-3/2) ([Komech & Kopylova 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.3799), READ intro), so the late field at r < 10 is a slow tail that a short layer neither absorbs nor leaves undisturbed. No source found for a reflection coefficient against k for this sponge near the gap, or for a rule on gamma_max against m. A plane-wave estimate (Addendum A1, N1, DERIVED) puts critical damping at gamma = 2 sqrt(k^2 + m^2), so gamma_max = 2 m is critical for the slowest modes; the project's own 1D scan has its knee at k0 between 0.4 and 0.7 (returned energy fraction 0.069 at 0.4, 0.0021 at 0.7, 1.9e-5 at 1.0, `PROJECT DATA`), and the F-001 onset times correspond to k of 0.50 to 0.56 (N2), the same band. Searches 21 to 28 and 36 in Addendum A3.
- Interpretation notes (scholar): (i) This is a known limitation class (slow waves near the mass gap), not a failure to absorb radiation of the kind Step 2 produces: the k0 = 2.4 result stands. (ii) The error over initial norm is 0.060; read as an amplitude ratio the energy-like fraction is about 4e-3 (DERIVED, rough: the state norm is not the energy). The 1.744 is large because the reference signal is 3 to 6 percent of its start, which is the t^(-3/2) tail. (iii) The literature validates absorbers on a plateau-supporting target (shell energy during an oscillon plateau), a better-conditioned test for Step 2 than relative L2 of a free tail; the box-doubling rule in the plan is compatible with it. (iv) The 22 interior radius is far smaller than published practice (20 oscillon scales, 50 to 100 from the core); that is a cost choice in 3D, but it means a Step 2 run that releases slow quanta while it settles ([Zhang et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.01202), footnote 15, READ, describes them) will see them again unless the interior is large. (v) The 1D scan already flagged that below k0 of about 0.5 its number is not a reflection coefficient, so the design was evidently chosen with fast radiation in mind (the comment above `SPONGE_DEFAULT` in `swirl/engine/evolver.py` says as much).
- Cross-reference: `PRIOR_ART.md` section 6 (Boundaries; the slow-quanta bullet anticipated the problem in qualitative form), Addendum A1 and A3; SESSION_01 report section: not yet written; reviewer finding: none supplied to the scholar.
- Status: explained (qualitatively, by a known limitation and by the project's own 1D data). Open as a design constraint on the Step 2 box, an orchestrator decision; rerun not needed.

For 90-degree steps the case is special in the published models too: DK 2002 find that for m = 2 bound states exist only for theta = 4 pi/N of at most pi/2 (N of at least 8), and show an exact rotating stationary cluster for N = 8, m = 2 (theta = pi/2), equilibrium radius R_0 = 5.5, angular velocity 0.092, in their 2D saturable model with a complex field (READ, Fig. 4c); [Dong, Liu, Malomed 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22231) (READ) state that steps up to 90 degrees attract and steps between 90 and 270 degrees repel, find a minimum R_min of the ansatz energy for steps up to 90 degrees, and find for steps between 90 and 270 degrees a global minimum only at infinite radius (expansion).
