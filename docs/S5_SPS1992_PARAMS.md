## 1. What the abstract says (ABSTRACT)

Source: [APS abstract page](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.46.6161), fetched twice through the fetch tool. The second fetch returned the abstract sentence by sentence (an exact opening fragment of each sentence plus a paraphrase of the rest). What follows is my paraphrase of those nine sentences, with the numbers and technical terms exactly as the tool returned them; I did not see the full wording of every sentence.

1. Earlier theory and molecular dynamics showed that a periodic one-dimensional lattice with nearest-neighbour quadratic and quartic interactions supports stationary localized modes.
2. The most localized ones are an odd-parity mode with displacement pattern A(..., 0, -1/2, 1, -1/2, 0, ...) and an even-parity mode with pattern A(..., 0, -1, 1, 0, ...), A being the amplitude.
3. These patterns become exact for the pure even-order anharmonic lattice as the order increases.
4. Both in that limit and for the harmonic plus quartic lattice, the odd-parity mode is unstable to infinitesimal perturbations and the even-parity mode is stable.
5. For the pure quartic lattice the predicted growth rate of the instability is 0.15 in units of the mode frequency, in excellent agreement with the rate seen in their molecular dynamics.
6. In contrast, the even-parity mode persists unchanged for more than 32 000 mode oscillations in their simulations.
7. The simulations show that the instability does not destroy the odd mode but makes it move.
8. A smoothly travelling version of these modes is also discussed.
9. As they move, these modes have a phase difference between adjacent relative displacements that is not constant, unlike travelling modes described earlier by others.

Reading of the pieces, with the tag and what is inference:

- **Pattern naming.** Odd-parity = (-1/2, 1, -1/2) = centred on a site = the plan's single bump (Sievers-Takeno). Even-parity = (-1, 1) = centred between two sites = the plan's S-bend (Page). `ABSTRACT` (sentence 2). Careful: other authors use the opposite words for the same patterns. Duran et al. describe the site-centred Sievers-Takeno mode as having a displacement that is even about its centre and the bond-centred Page mode as having an odd displacement ([arXiv:2111.09167](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.09167), sec. 2, `READ`). Use the patterns, not the words (standard 8).
- **"Periodic lattice".** In the title and in sentence 1 this most likely means a translation-periodic chain (a crystal), not periodic boundary conditions. Boundary conditions of their runs: no source found.
- **Lattice and potential.** Nearest-neighbour harmonic plus quartic; also the pure quartic case; also, as a limit, pure even-order potentials of increasing order. `ABSTRACT`. The constants (K2, K4, any factor 1/4 in front of the quartic term), the mass and the normalisation: no source found. A thesis summarising the paper calls the lattice a beta-FPU lattice ([Butt thesis, Nottingham 2006](https://repository.nottingham.ac.uk/server/api/core/bitstreams/68b4dd6c-a2ba-46fd-81a5-04b5aae4d720/content), sec. 1.3.1, `SECONDHAND`, `READ` of the thesis).
- **Anharmonicity value (beta A^2, K4 A^2/K2) and mode frequency over band maximum:** no source found, for every run in the paper. Two remarks that are `DERIVED`, not from the paper: (a) in the pure quartic lattice there is no linear term, so there is no band maximum and no dimensionless anharmonicity; the problem is scale-free (rescaling the amplitude rescales the frequency), which is why a single growth rate "in units of the mode frequency" can be quoted; (b) in the harmonic plus quartic lattice the combination is K4 A^2 / K2, and the mode frequency over the band maximum goes to 1 as it goes to 0.
- **Chain length, boundary conditions, time step, integrator:** no source found.
- **How the initial condition was made:** no source found. The abstract writes the modes as A times a fixed pattern, which suggests the molecular dynamics started from the pattern, but this is my inference, not a statement in the abstract. Page's earlier paper builds both patterns as rotating-wave solutions (triatomic and diatomic molecule patterns) that become exact as the anharmonic order rises ([PRB 41, 7835 (1990)](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.41.7835), `ABSTRACT`).
- **"32,000 cycles".** Sentence 6: the even-parity mode persists unchanged for more than 32 000 mode oscillations, `ABSTRACT`. The cycle count is in oscillations of the mode itself (periods of its own frequency). Which lattice this run used is not said. The sentence starts "In contrast" right after the pure-quartic growth-rate sentence, so it most naturally refers to the same molecular-dynamics runs, but that is an inference. "Unchanged" is the abstract's word; the criterion (amplitude, centre, energy window) is not given.
- **How the odd mode's instability shows.** (i) Predicted growth rate for the pure quartic lattice: 0.15 in units of the mode frequency, in agreement with the molecular dynamics (sentence 5). The abstract does not say whether "growth rate" is an e-folding rate per unit time in units of the angular frequency or something else. `DERIVED` consequence of the first reading: Floquet multiplier per period exp(2 pi x 0.15) = 2.57, an e-fold in about 1.06 periods. If the unit is the ordinary frequency the number would be 2 pi times larger. Do not use 0.15 as a pass/fail number until the reading is fixed. (ii) For the harmonic plus quartic lattice: unstable, no growth rate given in the abstract. (iii) The instability moves the mode instead of destroying it (sentence 7). Growth rate for finite K4 A^2/K2: no source found; other authors find the real multiplier is close to 1 near the band edge and grows with frequency ([Sanchez-Rey et al., arXiv:nlin/0306025](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0306025), sec. II, `READ`).
- **That it starts to move, and how fast.** Secondhand: Flach and Willis say Sandusky, Page and Schmidt observed this relation by measuring the velocity v and the modulation frequency omega_3 independently in careful numerical experiments (their Figs. 7 and 8), with omega_3 = 2 pi v, v in lattice spacings per unit time and omega_3 the frequency of the modulation of the moving object ([arXiv:patt-sol/9704004](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9704004), sec. IX C, `READ`, `SECONDHAND` for the SPS figures). Velocities and the number of sites crossed: no source found. The same review describes the paper as a careful numerical study relating dynamically unstable breathers to moving breather-like objects (sec. IX A, `READ`).
- **Smoothly travelling mode (sentences 8 and 9).** A travelling version of the modes in which the phase difference between adjacent relative displacements is not constant. `ABSTRACT`. Nothing about its energy loss, its speed range or its radiation is in the abstract. **It is not a statement about loss at low versus high speed.** No source found for that.

## 2. The 1994 companion (ABSTRACT)

Sandusky and Page, Phys. Rev. B 50, 866 (1994) ([APS abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.50.866)): earlier work found the zone-boundary phonon of a nearest-neighbour quartic plus quadratic chain unstable and linked this to localized modes; adding a cubic term still gives the instability but a large enough cubic term stabilises the zone-boundary mode; the instability is directly tied to existence of localized modes; a separate "period-doubling" zone-boundary instability occurs for Lennard-Jones, Morse and Born-Mayer chains, the localized-mode instability does not, and direct numerical searches found no localized modes for these potentials in monatomic lattices. `ABSTRACT`. Flach and Willis restate the cubic-term result: increasing |phi_3| from zero at fixed phi_4 > 0 loses the breathers that bifurcate from the upper band-edge plane wave exactly when the inequality 3 phi_2 phi_4 >= 4 phi_3^2 fails, "demonstrated by Sanduski and Page" ([arXiv:patt-sol/9704004](https://arxiv.org/abs/patt-sol/9704004), sec. X C, eq. (10.22) with v_4 = 0; `READ`, `SECONDHAND` for the attribution).

## 5. Helper algebra for the solver team (DERIVED, not literature; reviewer to check)

1. Zone-boundary plane wave u_n = (-1)^n a(t) in G2: the stretch is d_n = -2 (-1)^n a, and a obeys a_tt = -4a - 16 beta a^3. For a = A cos(omega t): omega^2 = 4 + 12 beta A^2 (A the displacement amplitude), or 4 + 3 beta s^2 with s = 2A the stretch amplitude.
2. The same wave in G1 (F odd): a_tt = -2 F(2a) = -4a/(1 - 4a^2). It is a one-degree-of-freedom oscillator with potential -(1/2) ln(1 - 4a^2) and a pole at a = 1/2 (stretch 1). Period T = 2 integral_0^{s_m} ds / sqrt(ln((1 - s^2)/(1 - s_m^2))), s the stretch, s_m its maximum. Frequency 2 at small amplitude, rising without bound as s_m goes to 1; to leading order omega approaches pi sqrt(ln(1/(1 - s_m^2))) (a hard-wall limit: speed sqrt(2E), walls at stretch plus and minus 1).
3. The small-amplitude existence test of James for a potential V with V''(0) = 1 is B = (1/2) V''''(0) - (V'''(0))^2 > 0, equivalently B = 3 K4 - 4 K3^2 for F = d + K3 d^2 + K4 d^3 ([Sanchez-Rey et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0306025) `READ`; same coefficient as 3 K2 K4 - 4 K3^2 in [Chong and Kevrekidis, arXiv:2408.15837](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15837), sec. 3, `READ`). G2: B = 3 beta. G1: F = d + d^3 + d^5 + ..., K3 = 0, K4 = 1, B = 3. So at small amplitude G1 is G2 with beta = 1.
4. Time for 32,000 cycles at breather frequency Omega: 2 pi (32,000)/Omega; at Omega = 4.5 that is about 44,700 time units; at Omega = 2.5, about 80,400.
