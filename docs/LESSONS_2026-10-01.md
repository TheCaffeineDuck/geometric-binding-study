## Physics lessons

### 1. The "oscillons" were free wave packets [audit]

- **What happened:** Gaussian packets at phi0 = 0.5 were treated as oscillons. They were not self-trapped.
- **Evidence:** the quartic and sextic terms hold about 0.06% of a packet's energy. The frequency used, 1.113, is above m = 1, and a self-trapped lump needs a frequency below m. In `set_B_baseline.json`, max|phi| falls to about 5% of phi0 by t = 50, matching free dispersal, (1 + t²/(m²R⁴))^(-3/4) = 4.4%. It then climbs back toward 24% by t = 500 because waves wrap around the periodic box.
- **Guard:** before trusting a run, report omega relative to m and the nonlinear energy fraction.

### 2. Binding energy was interference energy, fixed at t = 0 [audit]

- **What happened:** E_bind was read as evidence of bound states. It is the cross term of superposed Gaussians, conserved by Noether's theorem.
- **Evidence:** for binary phases s = ±1, with no free parameters:

```
E_bind ≈ sum over ALL pairs of s_i s_j J(d_ij)
J(d) = phi0^2 pi^(3/2) R^3 exp(-d^2/4R^2) [ m^2 + 3/(2R^2) - d^2/(4R^4) ]
```

  For continuous phases theta, each pair contributes `J(d) cos(theta_i) cos(theta_j) + K(d) sin(theta_i) sin(theta_j)` with `K(d) = phi0^2 omega_eff^2 pi^(3/2) R^3 exp(-d^2/4R^2)`.

  | Check | Closed form | Legacy PDE |
  |---|---|---|
  | Pair, d = 6 | 5.203 | 5.172 |
  | Pair, d = 10 | 0.239 | 0.239 |
  | Cube checkerboard | -51.65 | -51.53 |
  | Cube anti-tetrahedral | -23.98 | -23.94 |
  | Icosahedron all-same | 165.96 | 164.11 |
  | 50 random-phase seeds, T = 1000 | correlation 0.999997, largest miss 0.16 | range -40 to +78 |

- **Guard:** compute the analytic t = 0 value first. If the PDE adds nothing to it, the result is not dynamics.

### 3. The variance rule was second-neighbour pairs [audit]

- **What happened:** sigma²_NS was presented as a beyond-pairwise, many-body invariant. It is the second-neighbour pair sum in disguise.
- **Evidence:** at fixed cross-edge count the slope of E_bind against sigma²_NS is N·J(d2) on the icosahedron and cube, and (N/2)·J(d2) on the octahedron. Predicted 3.83, 7.61, 2.85. Measured 3.79, 7.64, 2.81. The cube's 19% "many-body" deviation is face and body diagonals. The truncated octahedron breaks the rule because square diagonals and hexagon second neighbours sit at different distances.
- **Guard:** before claiming a many-body effect, sum over all pairs, not only edges.

### 4. Energies were compared at fixed amplitude, not fixed N

- **What happened:** same-phase pairs were called repulsive because their superposition has more energy. That is more field, not a force.
- **Guard:** compare at fixed adiabatic invariant N. Measure force from centroid acceleration.

### 5. Set B and Set C do not have phi = 0 as the true vacuum

- **What happened:** V stays non-negative only if g4² <= 1.6 g6 m². Set B has 0.090 against 0.088. Set C has 0.25 against 0.16.
- **Guard:** require beta = g6 m²/g4² >= 5/8 for production parameters.

### 6. NEST k-essence: the soliton is not there

- **No regular solution:** for static, spherically symmetric fields define W = (1/2)phi'² + 3 kappa phi'⁴ - V(phi). The field equation gives dW/dr = -(2/r) phi'² (1 + 4 kappa phi'²) <= 0. A smooth origin has phi'(0) = 0, so W(0) = -V <= 0. At infinity W = 0. A non-increasing function cannot rise from a non-positive value to zero unless it is constant, so phi is constant. Finite-energy profiles that satisfy the virial identity must have a kink at the origin, which is a point source.
- **Sign problem:** the +kappa|grad phi|⁴ that beats Derrick also gives H a term -3 kappa phi_t⁴ and a propagation speed along gradients of sqrt((1 + 12 kappa phi'²)/(1 + 4 kappa phi'²)) > 1.
- **Own data:** the k-essence sweep found 0 of 30 binding configurations.
- **Guard:** Derrick's condition is necessary, not sufficient. Check regularity at the origin, hyperbolicity, and energy boundedness for time-dependent fields.
- **Not yet checked:** the golden profile files were not available for review. Whoever has them should look at phi'(0).

## Code and analysis lessons

### 7. The "dissipation" term was not dissipation [audit]

- **What happened:** `engine/evolver.py` adds sigma·dx⁴·k⁴·phi to the acceleration. On phi that is a conservative stiffness term. `compute_energy` leaves it out.
- **Evidence:** the isolated energy rises from 13.46178 to 13.46374 within the first 10 time units, then stays flat. That step is the paper's "0.015% radiation drift". The sigma = 0 comparison in §8.7 compared two conservative systems.
- **Guard:** the energy function includes every term in the equation of motion. Test any damping by checking that energy falls monotonically.

### 8. Self-organization was the initial draw [audit]

- **What happened:** "60% of seeds become bound within 177 periods" was read as a dynamical attractor.
- **Evidence:** in `outputs/phase12`, 30 of 50 seeds are bound at t = 0 and the same 30 at T = 1000. No seed changes sign. The largest change in E_bind is 0.03. The order parameter S(t) is read at vertex grid points after the packets have dispersed.
- **Guard:** check the t = 0 value of any statistic described as evolved. A phase diagnostic needs a localization check alongside it.

### 9. The collective periods were FFT bins [audit]

- **What happened:** periods of 1010, 505 and 252 were reported as collective breathing modes.
- **Evidence:** the series has 101 samples at spacing 10, length 1010. The three periods are the window length divided by 1, 2 and 4.
- **Guard:** a spectral peak in the lowest few bins is not a measurement.

### 10. Symmetry classes left out exact symmetries [audit]

- **What happened:** the icosahedron enumeration used 60 rotations plus the global flip and omitted reflections. The cube analysis omitted the flip phi -> -phi, which is exact because V is even.
- **Evidence:** class counts at half cross-edges:

  | Group | Icosahedron (CE = 15) | Cube (CE = 6) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Rotations only | 12 | 7 |
  | Rotations and reflections | 10 | 6 |
  | Rotations and flip | 6 | 5 |
  | All three | 5 | 4 |

  Icosahedron classes D and E (both -1.494) are very likely the mirror pair. The cube "C≡D" equivalence is probably a flip or mirror pair. The C and D arrays were not available to confirm.
- **Guard:** use rotations, reflections and the field sign flip together.

### 11. Robustness tests that could not fail

- **What happened:** Set C, N = 128, L = 100, sigma = 0 and T = 2000 all confirmed the results. In the linear regime none of them could have done otherwise.
- **Guard:** each robustness test states in advance what failure would look like.

### 12. Calibrated quantities reported as measured

- **What happened:** omega_eff = 1.113 was set by matching energies in `10_continuous_phase.py`. The paper calls it a measured oscillation frequency. At quadratic order it equals sqrt(m² + 3/(2R²)) = 1.1136.
- **Guard:** label every quantity as measured, calibrated or derived.

### 13. Labels instead of coordinates

- **What happened:** enumeration scripts and PDE runs used different cube vertex orderings, which corrupted sigma²_NS labels.
- **Guard:** compute structural quantities from vertex positions.

## Process lessons

### 14. Story before data

- **What happened:** results were framed before the cheapest falsifying test was run. The literature review ranked the "adiabatic invariant" first for novelty before it had been measured. The December 2025 report of 316 out of 316 bound pairs used the same kind of packet and has not been re-examined.
- **Guard:** run the test most likely to kill the claim first. Write verdict criteria before running. No paper drafting until the map is complete.
