# Swirl Theory: the rules of the medium

Date: 2026-11-11
Status: agreed with Aaron line by line in chat on 2026-11-11. The twist is parked.
Purpose: one reference for what the theory says, in Aaron's words, so that plans and models are built on his picture and not on an assumed one. Where this sheet and an older document disagree, this sheet wins.

## The medium

1. Space is a medium. It does not flow, it is uniform at rest, and it is always three-dimensional.
2. It is elastic, with a maximum squeeze and a maximum pull. Approaching either, tension climbs toward infinity. It is a limit, not a hard wall.
3. At the limit the medium is rigid. Nothing propagates through it. A wave reaching it bounces back.
4. Squeeze and pull come in equal and opposite pairs. As squeeze grows in one place, pull occurs around it.
5. Away from the limits, space is a free elastic medium and waves travel freely.
6. Waves move at one fixed speed through the medium itself. The speed is fixed per unit of medium, not per unit of distance.
7. No numbers are inserted. Light speed and Planck's constant are the hard stops on what the medium can do. Constants are used as units and results are relationships. Lensing by gravity is one route to the elasticity of space, and other routes are open.

## Energy and swirls

8. There is no matter separate from energy. Everything is structured movement of energy in the medium.
9. Energy either travels as waves or is trapped in place as a swirl.
10. A swirl is too short and too fast for the medium to carry. It has an optimal length and rate. Too long, or moving too fast, and it becomes a travelling wave.
11. A swirl has a fixed length that cannot change. When it bows, its ends are drawn in and space moves around it. That pull is always inward, whichever way it bows.
12. The pull stretches the surrounding medium to its limit and forms a rigid gap. The gap is the wall that holds the wave. The surface of the swirl is pulling against a medium at its limit.
13. A swirl cannot decay, because nothing smaller exists to radiate into. A lone swirl persists until something acts on it. It can be dissipated or absorbed.
14. The objects:
    - **Pinch.** Space squeezes toward a center and springs back. Three moments: maximum squeeze, neutral, maximum stretch. Maximum squeeze equals maximum stretch. At the neutral instant nothing is there in space.
    - **Single bump.** One hump, round. Probably not stable enough to build a structure. A stepping stone.
    - **S-bend.** Two halves moving opposite ways with a still center. A more stable trap than the single bump. It has dimples at its ends where the wave meets the gap.
    - **Twist.** An S-bend with a sideways motion added, out of step with the up-and-down motion. Parked. See the section on parked and open items.

## Structures and mass

15. Swirls can merge, and they can also hold together through their geometry. Once bonded, a group withstands dissipation and absorption, and only something specific can break it. Bonded groups are what we observe as particles.
16. A structure's field reaches further than a lone swirl's, through the same pull as rules 17 and 18. One pluck makes no sound. Many together make a sound that reaches further.
17. Trapped energy contracts the medium. Contracted medium is denser. Denser space is a gravity well, and mass is that attraction. More energy, more mass.
18. The pull spreads over a growing surface with distance, so it weakens.

## Time and motion

19. Time is cycles. Everything has a cycle, and as cycles complete, time has passed.
20. A swirl's own cycle is not affected by dense space, because it is too small to ride on another wave. A clock is a larger object, and its tick is an event: something moving across a span. In dense space that span holds more medium, so the event takes longer to finish. The clock runs slower.
21. Anyone inside dense space cannot notice, because they are made of the same medium.
22. A particle never leaves its wave state. It does not move through the medium. It is a configuration of waves propagating in it. This is the answer to the ether experiments.
23. A moving structure's events take longer, because its internal waves must cross more medium to reach a part that is moving away. This depends on speed, not on acceleration.

One principle covers rules 20 and 23: an event takes longer when its waves must cross more medium.

## The black hole, as the same structure at its limit

- The core is at maximum squeeze.
- At its horizon the pull is at maximum. This is the rigid gap, where nothing can exist or move.
- Beyond the gap is a shell where the pull traps energy again.
- Further out the pull is spread over more surface, and growth and contraction balance. Some radiation escapes while more is absorbed.
- Something approaching the gap travels through space so dense that it takes a very long time to move.

## Notes on where pieces come from

- Rule 11 rests on Aaron's statement that a sine wave contains more arc length than the straight line between its endpoints. A fixed-length wave that bows must draw its ends together. The pull grows with the square of the amplitude, as the energy of a vibration does, so contraction proportional to trapped energy (rule 17) follows from the geometry.
- Rule 23 was proposed by Claude and accepted by Aaron. The result is Lorentz's, from 1904. It is used as a check on the model, not claimed as new.
- An earlier inference by Claude that the medium must resist stretch more than squeeze is withdrawn. Rule 11 does that job without it.

## Parked and open

- **The twist and its timing.** Parked. A possible factor to revisit once the overall structure and group behavior are understood, or if the work hits a wall.
- **What connects two swirls created together.** Open. Measurements on paired particles show a link that acts faster than waves travel (the question raised by the Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen paper). Rule 6 alone cannot give that. Places to look: rule 3 and rule 4. Not a target until structures exist.

## Stated earlier, not yet reviewed against this sheet

- The mass gap is a missing accounting of energy.
- The gluon is not a particle. It is the interaction between swirl structures.
- Between swirls only a weak force exists, like the van der Waals force in helium, and nothing smaller. Larger structures obey the nuclear forces.

## What has been tested so far, and against which rules

- Sessions 1 to 4 tested a swirl trapped below a lowest note, in a medium with one number per point. That is not the trap in rules 10 to 12.
- Session 5 tested a trap above a highest note on a string that could only move sideways. On a grid the S-bend held without loss and the single bump turned into an S-bend. Without a grid nothing held. That string had no way to pull medium inward, so it could not form the gap in rule 12.
- No session has yet tested rules 11, 12, 17, 18, 20 or 23.
